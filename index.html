What is actually learning?

Each car has its own online action-value learner. Experience changes 64 coefficients that score contact, positioning, boost-route and support plans. Candidate-specific features include route moisture, braking risk, wind, resource charge and engine heat. Contact candidates vary approach pace, braking, offset and commitment. A predictive planner and motor controller supply the basic driving.

In 2v2, Mica and Slate face Ember and Sienna. Each car has separate weights; rewards for goals and useful touches are shared with its teammate. Reach-time assignments, support positions and pass candidates are authored. Twelve additional features let tactical preferences adapt to team context. A received pass is counted only after two same-team physical contacts, separated by more than six world units and less than six seconds; opponent contact breaks the sequence. Passes themselves earn no bonus.

The observer clusters three-part approach sequences before inspecting outcomes, including missed opportunities. It compares positive progress against other attempts in the same wetness, threat and height context. Its 95% Wilson intervals are descriptive: self-play attempts are not independent experiments. Even a learning-associated label is not causal proof or a generalisation claim.

Frozen evaluation uses two surface conditions, mirrored sides and archived opponents. Four matched starts per agent (or per team in 2v2) are a small sample. The observer never moves a car or modifies rewards. Weather and engine heat survive goals and two-minute round boundaries.

This is an original car-soccer experiment, not Rocket League or a trained Rocket League bot.
